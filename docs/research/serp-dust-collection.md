# serp-dust-collection

## best-dust-collectors-for-small-shops
- Top guides lead with shop vac versus dedicated collector, then CFM, filtration (1 micron) and mobile versus wall mount; WEN DC3401, Shop Fox, Jet, Laguna, Ridgid shop vac recur.
- Advice: match the collector to tool, hose size and power before chasing a headline airflow number; small units may not run large planers or drum sanders.
- Wall units save floor space; some cautions about heavy units hard to move in small shops.
- Many guides affiliate led and some dated.
- Missed angle: free air CFM versus airflow after hose loss, static pressure, cyclone pre separation to keep filters clear, fine dust below 10 microns needs good filtration and a respirator, bag capacity versus filling frequency, noise.
## best-workshop-air-filtration-systems
- Top guides lead with CFM, filter micron rating and shop size.
- They split picks by budget, HEPA, large shop and ceiling-hung units.
- They note air filtration differs from a dust collector and suggest matching CFM to room size.
- Gap we cover: air changes per hour, California regulation notes, filter cost and noise, and that a unit is not a respirator.

## best-safety-glasses-for-woodworking
- Top guides lead with ANSI Z87.1 and Z87+ marking, lens material and anti-fog coating, then fit and side coverage.
- Picks are split by general shop use, wearers of prescription glasses and dusty tasks that need sealed goggles.
- Common questions: do I need Z87+, will they fog, can I wear them over glasses.
- Missed angle: over-glasses styles do not seal against fine dust, and anti-fog coatings wear off.
- Honest tradeoff: sealed goggles fog more and feel warmer; open frames are more comfortable but let in fine dust.

## best-respirators-for-sanding
- Top guides lead with filter class (N95, P100), reusable versus disposable, and whether the product is NIOSH approved.
- Picks are split by drywall and wood sanding, painting and finishes, and heavy dust.
- Common questions: N95 or P100, how to check fit, how often to replace filters.
- Missed angle: fit and seal check, facial hair, and the difference between NIOSH and European P2 or FFP2 marks.
- Honest tradeoff: disposables are cheap but seal less well; reusable masks need filters and cleaning; P2 kits are not NIOSH approved.
